# The Cormorant Tide

## Synopsis

When a local historian is found dead on the tidal causeway to St Osyth's Eye, Detective Inspector Rowan Bell returns to the Sussex harbour town she spent years trying to leave behind. The death looks like a cruel accident until the tide board, a missing notebook, and an old lifeboat disaster point towards a murder planned by someone who knows the coast too well.

Rowan's investigation moves through harbour sheds, cliff paths, museum archives, and the crumbling island house where cormorants roost at low water. The closer she comes to the truth, the more Pelham Hard's old loyalties close around her, and the more clearly she sees that the town's most honoured story was built on a lie.

## Tone & Style

Restrained, atmospheric, and clue-forward. The prose should carry the salt, damp wool, gull noise, pub heat, and uneasy intimacy of a small English coastal town without becoming florid. The mystery should feel fair: clues are visible, suspects have credible pressures, and the ending resolves the murder and the older secret.
